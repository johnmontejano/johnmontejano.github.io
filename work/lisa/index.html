LISA · PRODUCT AND WEB DESIGN · INDEPENDENT ACCELERATOR

LISA: making a complex program clear enough to act on.

Role
Product and web design
Scope
Site structure, surfaces, and the path to application
Context
Independent 12-week accelerator for early-stage founders
Site
Visit the LISA site (opens in new tab)

The design problem

LISA is an independent 12-week accelerator for early-stage founders. Programs like this carry real structural complexity: cohorts, curriculum, mentorship, events, and an application path.

That leaves the website doing heavy lifting. It has to explain what the program is, who it is for, and what to do next, without burying a prospective founder in detail. That was the brief.

LISA is an independent program created and operated by alumni of the LEAD program. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by Stanford University or Stanford Graduate School of Business.

What I owned

Product and web design, including the website itself: the structure, the surfaces, and the path from "what is this?" to a submitted application.

The design decisions

Subtract before adding

The work was an exercise in subtraction. The question was not what could be said about the program, but what a first-time visitor needs in the first minute. Everything that failed that test moved down the page or off it.

Sequence the story so each section earns the next

What LISA is comes before how it works, and how it works comes before what it asks of you. Each section answers the question the previous one raises, which is why the program's structure reads as a sequence rather than a specification.

Keep one action in reach the whole way

A program with this many moving parts can offer a visitor a dozen things to click. There is one action that matters, apply, and it stays reachable at every point in the story instead of waiting at the end.

Make a twelve-week structure scannable

A founder deciding whether to commit needs to locate themselves in the program before they read it line by line. The structure is laid out in phases that can be scanned first and read second, with the commitments stated plainly rather than softened.

Why this is the same work

The same discipline I apply to workflows, pointed at a surface: remove steps, clarify handoffs, make the next action obvious.

An application path and a job intake are the same object seen from different sides. Both are a sequence of handoffs where a person can drop out, and both get better by removing steps rather than decorating them.

The evidence

The story, sequenced

What LISA is, then how it works: cohorts, mentorship, curriculum, and the pitch event, each answering the question the previous section raises.

Structure made scannable

A twelve-week program compressed into a scan: phases a founder can locate themselves in, and the commitments stated plainly.

What this proves

Design is the supporting half of my offer. When the confusing part of a business is the part customers see, I fix that surface with the same systems thinking.

The program site is public, so the structure and the application path can be read end to end: gsb-lead-lisa.com.
