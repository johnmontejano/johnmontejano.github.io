GELATOTECH · OWNER-OPERATOR · MOBILE IPHONE REPAIR, SAN FRANCISCO

GelatoTech: every role in the business, including the one holding the screwdriver.

Role
Owner-operator
Scope
Website, SEO, Google and Facebook ads, operations, repairs
Context
Mobile iPhone repair, San Francisco
Site
Visit gelatotech.com (opens in new tab)

What was happening

A broken phone is urgent, and the standard fix was built for the shop, not the customer. Drive somewhere. Hand over the phone. Wait.

GelatoTech inverted that. The technician comes to you and repairs it on the spot. A model like that lives or dies on coordination: bookings, routes, parts, timing, and what has to happen after the job.

What I owned

All of it. I built and ran the website, did the SEO, ran the Google and Facebook advertising, managed operations, and did the repairs myself.

Running the whole loop

Owning every role meant seeing the whole loop most consultants only see a slice of. Each stage hands off to the next, and every handoff is a place a job can stall.

  • What the ad promises. The claim that brings someone in sets what the rest of the business has to deliver.
  • What the booking asks. Every extra question is a customer you can lose, and every question you skip is work you pick up later.
  • What the schedule can absorb. On-site repair means the calendar and the map are the same problem.
  • What happens at the door. The part the customer actually judges you on.
  • What happens afterwards. The difference between one repair and a referral.

What I took from it

The lessons arrived in the operator's version, not the consultant's version. When the call came in mid-repair, the cost of missing it was mine, so I learned it the way owners learn it.

A missed call is a lost job. Follow-up is a system or it doesn't happen. The owner's time is the scarcest part in the machine.

Those three sentences are why the customer-facing surface and the operation behind it get designed together in everything I do now. On this business they were the same job, because I was doing both.

The evidence

The coordination model

Book, arrive, pay when it's fixed. Three steps for the customer, and a chain of scheduling, routing, and follow-up behind each one.

The booking surface

The instant-price picker I ran the business on: a customer picks a device and gets a price without a phone call. Acquisition and intake, designed as one surface.

What this proves

When I assess your operation, I am not imagining your day. I've dispatched myself, quoted at the curb, and invoiced at the kitchen table. That's the eye you're hiring.

The business is still public, so you can look at the surface I built and the booking flow I ran it on: gelatotech.com.
