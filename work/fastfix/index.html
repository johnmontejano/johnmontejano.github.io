FASTFIX · OWNER · PLATFORM FOR HOME-SERVICE BUSINESSES

FastFix: the platform version of everything I'd learned running a service business.

Scope

Context

What was happening

Home-service businesses (plumbers, HVAC techs, repair crews) win work by being good at the work. The business around the work is what wears an owner down: calls to answer, jobs to schedule, quotes to chase, reviews to request, invoices to send.

I had run a service business myself, so I knew where those hours went. They had been mine.

Instead of automating one business at a time, build the platform that handles the busywork for the trade.

What I owned

FastFix is my company. I own the website, the marketing, the product, the growth, and the automation: the storefront and the machinery behind it. There is no version of this project where someone else made the important decisions.

The decisions behind it

Build the platform, not one more one-off automation

Consulting fixes one operation at a time. A platform addresses the same pattern across a trade. That is the trade-off I took on deliberately: a platform is slower to build than a single automation, and it only earns its keep if the same workflows genuinely repeat from one business to the next.

Own the surface and the system as one thing

The website, the marketing, and the automation behind them are usually three different vendors making three different assumptions. Here they are one decision. What the site promises is exactly what the system underneath has to deliver, so the promise and the machinery get designed together.

Take the workflows in the order an owner feels them

The product is organized around the jobs owners actually absorb, taken one workflow at a time rather than all at once. The call. The schedule. The quote. The finished job. Each one is a manual task I have watched eat an owner's day, designed out of the business.

What changed

  • Calls answered and booked around the clock.
  • Scheduling without a wall calendar.
  • Quotes with follow-up built in, not remembered.
  • Finished jobs that become reviews and repeat work.

Those four are the shape of the product, and they are also the shape of the problem. Answering, scheduling, quoting, and closing the loop are the parts of a service business that depend on somebody remembering. Moving them out of memory and into a system is the whole idea.

The evidence

The workflow, productized

FastFix's own explanation of what it runs for an owner: leads, calls, closing, and day-to-day jobs. The manual steps of a service business, taken over one by one.

One system, not seven tools

The problem FastFix exists to end: job details scattered across inboxes, spreadsheets, and missed-call voicemail. One stack replaces the pile.

What this proves

This is what I bring to an assessment. Not a framework out of a book, but pattern recognition from building the system itself: where service businesses leak time, and which workflows repay automation first.

It is also checkable. FastFix is a live public product you can visit right now, at fastfix.ai.
