Who this is for

The right fit

Owner-run service businesses with real demand and not enough hours. Good work, heavy coordination.

Not the right fit

Teams shopping for a tool to bolt on. The tool comes last.

You'll recognize the symptoms.

  • Some leads quietly never get a reply.
  • Follow-up happens when someone remembers.
  • The same details retyped between phone, calendar, invoices.
  • Quotes go out. Then nothing, until you chase.
  • No-shows cost you mornings.
  • You are the system. When you're off, it's off.

What a workflow assessment includes

One structured conversation. We walk one real job end to end and map every step, tool, and handoff.

What I look at
  • How work arrivesCalls, forms, messages, referrals, and what happens to each.
  • Scheduling and dispatchHow a job gets a time, a place, and a person.
  • Data flowWhere customer and job details live, and how many times they get retyped.
  • Money pathQuote to approval to invoice to payment, and where it stalls.
  • Follow-upQuotes, reminders, reviews, rebooking. Everything that depends on memory today.
  • The owner's plateWhat only you can do, versus what you've simply always done.

What you leave with

By the end you'll know where your operation loses time, which of it is worth automating, what I'd fix first, and what I'd leave alone. That picture is yours to keep, whether or not we work together.

What a build aims for

  • Fewer handoffs between people and tools.
  • Follow-up on schedule, not on memory.
  • Details entered once, carried everywhere.
  • Reminders before every appointment.
  • Your evening back from admin.

When the website is part of the problem

Sometimes the friction starts on the surface: a booking path customers abandon, pages that make the wrong promise. I've designed and run my own customer-facing sites, so I can redesign yours as part of the same engagement.

How I work

  1. Observe

    Trace how work really moves. Not the org chart. The real path.

  2. Simplify

    Remove steps before automating them. Automated chaos is just faster chaos.

  3. Build

    Practical automation with the tools you already have.

  4. Improve

    Watch it run. Fix what breaks. Move to the next bottleneck.
